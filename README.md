Acadence
A clearer view of every academic week.
About the Project
Acadence is an academic workload visibility and coordination platform designed for students, instructors, and academic administrators. It brings academic deadlines and coursework into a clearer, more organized view so that demanding weeks can be recognized before they become overwhelming.

Instead of viewing every subject as if it exists on its own, Acadence aims to show how coursework across a student's academic section comes together throughout the semester. This shared perspective can help students prepare ahead, help instructors consider the workload already assigned to their classes, and help administrators identify periods when students may need better scheduling coordination.
Project Goal
The goal of Acadence is to make academic workload visible, understandable, and easier to coordinate across an academic semester.

Acadence aims to help prevent avoidable deadline pile-ups by giving students, instructors, and administrators a clearer picture of upcoming academic requirements. It encourages early preparation and informed scheduling rather than leaving students to discover conflicting deadlines only when they arrive.
The Problem We Address
Academic work is often planned one subject at a time. An instructor may set an examination or project deadline without having a clear view of the other major requirements students must complete during that same week. Students, meanwhile, may need to check several classes and platforms to understand everything that is due. Administrators may have limited visibility into workload patterns across sections and programs.

When these separate schedules come together, several major requirements can fall within the same week. This can create unnecessary pressure, reduce the time available for quality work, and make it harder for students to plan ahead.

Acadence addresses this problem by bringing available coursework into a consolidated view and presenting workload patterns in a way that can support better academic planning.
Who Acadence Is For
Students
Acadence helps students see upcoming coursework and deadlines in one place. Its timeline and weekly workload views are intended to help students recognize busy periods, understand what is coming up, and prepare earlier.
Instructors
Acadence gives instructors a view of coursework for the classes they handle. Instructors can review assignments, confirm how coursework is categorized, and consider the existing workload when planning academic requirements.
Academic Administrators
Acadence provides administrators with an overview of available workload information across connected classes and sections. These insights can help identify demanding periods and support conversations about scheduling and academic coordination.
What Acadence Aims to Provide
- A consolidated academic timeline — a clearer view of upcoming coursework and due dates.
- Weekly workload visibility — an at-a-glance indication of lighter and more demanding weeks.
- Instructor coursework review — a place for instructors to review classwork and confirm assessment categories.
- Academic workload insights — summarized views that help administrators recognize possible crunch weeks.
- Google Classroom connection — a way to bring authorized course and coursework information into the Acadence experience.
- Section-aware planning — a shared academic schedule for students who belong to the same section, with separate schedules for different sections.
How Acadence Understands Academic Sections
An academic section is a defined group of students, such as BSIT 3IS1, BSCS 1A, or BSInfoTech-WMAD 3A. Students in the same section generally share the same scheduled requirements for their common course offerings.

Acadence is designed around this shared-section model:
- Students in the same academic section share the section's recorded academic schedule.
- Subjects are treated as separate course offerings under that section.
- The section workload is considered across the course offerings recorded for that section.
- Different sections can have different course offerings and deadlines, even when they belong to the same program or year level.
This approach keeps the main workload view consistent for students who follow the same academic schedule. Individual workload differences are considered only when there is a genuine exception, such as an individually assigned requirement or a verified cross-section enrollment.
Our Intended Impact
Acadence aims to contribute to a more organized and considerate academic environment by helping the university community:
1. Recognize demanding weeks earlier so students have more time to prepare.
2. Encourage thoughtful deadline planning by making existing coursework easier for instructors to review.
3. Support academic coordination through a broader view of workload patterns across sections and programs.
4. Promote student well-being by helping reduce avoidable concentrations of major academic requirements.
5. Make workload discussions more informed through visible, understandable summaries of available academic information.
Our Guiding Principle
Make the workload visible before it becomes overwhelming.

Acadence is intended to support—not replace—the judgment of students, instructors, and administrators. Its purpose is to provide a clearer shared picture of academic workload so the people involved can make better-informed decisions throughout the semester.
